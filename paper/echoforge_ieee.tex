\title{EchoForge and Engineered Intelligence for Low-False-Alarm UAS Radar: A Strict-Open Synthetic Benchmark}

\author{Jeppson Taylor, Lead Author\\
NeverHuman Research Group}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Low-altitude uncrewed aerial system (UAS) radar detection is a rare-positive, false-alarm-constrained problem. EchoForge is a strict-open, radar-first synthetic sensing benchmark for fixed-wing pusher-prop public-proxy UAS research. It exposes public-source object priors, radar branch cards, synthetic complex IQ, range-Doppler products, acoustic and passive-RF cues, detector-view contracts, group-locked splits, and reproducible evidence without claiming measured-platform truth, proprietary-equivalent behavior, classified fidelity, or deployment performance. The benchmark run contains \MetricScenarioGroups{} scenario groups, \MetricPositiveGroups{} positive groups, and \MetricPhaseRecords{} phase records. Candidate discovery, calibration, thresholding, and model selection use train/CV rows only; the \MetricHoldoutRecords{}-record blind holdout is scored once after lock. The accepted comparator is the strongest human-engineered sensor-fusion lane assembled from the radar and cue branches. Engineered Intelligence (EI) improves point \PointKPI{} from \kpigain{\MetricPriorPointRecall{} to \MetricEiPointRecall{}} and improves \PrimaryKPI{} from \kpigain{\MetricPriorLcb{} to \MetricEiLcb{}}. It raises AP from \kpigain{\MetricPriorAP{} to \MetricEiAP{}} and cuts selected-threshold false positives from \kpigain{\MetricPriorSelectedFP{} to \MetricEiSelectedFP{}}. EI gives up global ROC AUC relative to prior fusion, \kpicaveat{\MetricEiRocAuc{} vs. \MetricPriorRocAuc{}}, so the claim is precise: EI improves the low-FPR operating point on this declared synthetic public-proxy benchmark, not universal ranking dominance or operational performance.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Synthetic radar, UAS detection, radar simulation, micro-Doppler, sensor fusion, engineered intelligence, low false alarm detection, reproducibility.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Radar detection for low-altitude UAS is difficult because the target is small, intermittent, and often embedded in clutter or hard-negative traffic that produces plausible false tracks. In this setting, ROC AUC is not enough. A detector can rank positives well on average and still fail once the false-positive budget is nearly exhausted. EchoForge therefore treats \PrimaryKPI{} as the claim-bearing metric, with AP, ROC AUC, calibration, selected-threshold confusion counts, and false-positive families as guardrails.

This paper has one experimental spine: simulator assumptions, human detector branches, accepted human fusion, EI search, selection lock, and one blind holdout pass. The paper-facing positive class is a fixed-wing pusher-prop public proxy. The proxy is inspired by open public descriptions of fixed-wing pusher-prop UAS families, but the benchmark does not claim measured Iranian-platform radar signatures, route behavior, payload behavior, fielded-system equivalence, or classified fidelity.

The contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a strict-open synthetic radar and multimodal sensing benchmark for fixed-wing pusher-prop public-proxy UAS scenarios;
  \item a radar-first evaluation setup connecting waveform assumptions, range-Doppler structure, CFAR/MTD scoring, micro-Doppler cues, tracking, and fusion;
  \item a human-method ladder that ends in accepted human sensor fusion before EI is introduced;
  \item an auditable EI method that evolves sparse calibrated fusion on train/CV evidence and is judged against the accepted comparator under one group-locked protocol;
  \item a focused figure set: simulator stack, phase ladder, EI train/CV evolution, false-positive burden, and qualitative range-Doppler examples.
\end{itemize}

\section{Radar Processing Background}
The radar branch begins with a received-power proxy,
\begin{equation}
P_r \propto \frac{P_tG_tG_r\lambda^2\sigma(\theta,f,\phi)}{(4\pi)^3R^4L},
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ is an aspect- and frequency-dependent public-proxy RCS term, $R$ is slant range, and $L$ is aggregate loss. EchoForge then synthesizes complex IQ as a target return plus clutter, interference, and receiver impairment,
\begin{equation}
x_{m,n}=a_{m,n}e^{j(2\pi f_dmT_r+\varphi_n)}+c_{m,n}+\epsilon_{m,n}.
\end{equation}
The range-Doppler budget follows standard radar processing: bandwidth controls range resolution, CPI controls Doppler resolution, and PRF constrains ambiguity. The derived quantities used in the branch cards are
\begin{equation}
\Delta R=\frac{c}{2B},\qquad \Delta f_D=\frac{1}{T_{\mathrm{CPI}}},\qquad \Delta v=\frac{\lambda}{2T_{\mathrm{CPI}}}.
\end{equation}

Classical detector families then ask whether a cell, Doppler ridge, or short track is locally exceptional. CFAR compares a peak against local clutter; MTD emphasizes coherent motion; track scoring rewards persistence; micro-Doppler features summarize modulation from rotating or flapping structures; and fusion combines sensor-local evidence under uncertainty \cite{skolnik2008,richards2014,rohling1983,chen2011,tahmoush2015,hall1997,barshalom2001}. Public measured radar datasets and UAS micro-Doppler studies are useful as observable-family anchors, but EchoForge uses them as compare-only references rather than as proof of measured positive-class truth \cite{kth2021,openradar2023,raddar2019,rahman2018,molchanov2014,ritchie2017}.

The simulator exposes six capabilities needed for this benchmark:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textit{Public-proxy object priors}: fixed-wing pusher-prop geometry, broad kinematics, aspect/RCS uncertainty, pusher-prop cadence, phase labels, and hard-negative families.
  \item \textit{Radar branch cards}: X/Ku C-UAS, S-band surveillance, and GBAD cueing archetypes with explicit carrier, bandwidth, CPI, PRF/CRF, chirp count, range resolution, Doppler resolution, and velocity resolution.
  \item \textit{Scene and receiver stress}: non-Gaussian clutter, weather/terrain glint, vegetation motion, RFI bursts, multipath ghosts, AGC compression, drift, quantization, dropped CPI, Doppler folding, and calibration offset.
  \item \textit{Multimodal cue streams}: acoustic cadence/agreement and passive-RF absence, contamination, sparse cue geometry, and provenance quality.
  \item \textit{Detector-view contracts}: model-facing features include detector scores and cue summaries; labels, split fields, group IDs, audit headers, and generator internals are denied to the model.
  \item \textit{Reproducible evidence}: deterministic seeds, group-locked splits, strict train/CV selection, one blind-holdout score pass, and generated figure/code appendices.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Defensive Jamming and Deception Stress Modeling}
EchoForge includes a bounded public taxonomy for radar jamming and deception stress, using public descriptions only as a high-level taxonomy seed \cite{radarJammingDeceptionWiki}. Mechanical-like stressors create cloud or decoy-like false-return pressure; noise-like electronic stressors create spot, sweep, barrage, or pulse-cover receiver contamination; deception-like stressors create repeater-like false-target and signature-augmentation artifacts. The activation decision is sampled at the scenario-group level, independently of label and split, so jamming/deception cannot become a shortcut for negatives. Detector features are IQ-derived summaries such as spectral flatness, range-line occupancy, pulse burstiness, ghost-peak count, and low-Doppler cloud mass.

\section{Benchmark Design}
The benchmark run contains \MetricScenarioGroups{} scenario groups, \MetricPositiveGroups{} positive groups, and \MetricPhaseRecords{} phase records. Each group expands into three fixed windows: initial take-up (0--30~s), climb transition (30--90~s), and cruise altitude (90--150~s). Split assignment occurs at the scenario-group level before phase expansion; all phase rows from the same group inherit the same split. The blind holdout contains \MetricHoldoutRecords{} records, including \MetricHoldoutPositiveRecords{} positive records from \MetricHoldoutPositiveGroups{} positive scenario groups.

The primary KPI is \PrimaryKPI{}. It is the lower 95\% group-block bootstrap bound for recall at FPR $\leq1\%$. The point estimate \PointKPI{} is also reported, but \PrimaryKPI{} is claim-bearing because the positive holdout is group-locked and small. Selected-threshold TP/FP/FN counts are kept separate from swept fixed-FPR metrics.

\section{Human Detection Ladder}
The human ladder is ordered from sensor-local evidence to accepted sensor fusion. Every method uses the same three phase windows and train/CV-only threshold or calibration basis. Figure~\ref{fig:phaseladder} repeats that method order across the three phases.

Table~\ref{tab:humanmethods} gives the compact processing contract; the prose below expands each rung so the ladder can be audited as a set of distinct human methods rather than as a single catch-all ``baseline.''

The first rung is the high-resolution X/Ku branch. It is the closest thing in EchoForge to a radar-local sanity check because it preserves the most discriminative structure in the synthetic IQ stream. Human review on this branch starts with CFAR-like power contrast, then checks whether the returned energy is concentrated in a plausible range-Doppler cell, and finally looks for micro-Doppler structure that is consistent with the public proxy rather than with clutter, birds, or an RC aircraft \cite{skolnik2008,richards2014,rohling1983,chen2011,tahmoush2015,rahman2018,molchanov2014}. This is a detector-view rung, not a claim-making rung: it answers whether the proxy is separable under a specific waveform and stress family, while still denying the analyst access to labels, split keys, group IDs, or holdout rows.

The second radar rung is the lower-resolution S-band branch. It gives up fine range detail in exchange for broader surveillance coverage, so the human decision relies more on motion-trend summaries, track persistence, and range-velocity coherence than on the high-resolution branch features \cite{schleher1999,richards2014,blackman1999,barshalom2001}. The GBAD cueing branch is even further from raw detection. It treats the radar as a source of cue confidence, horizon status, revisit cadence, and stale-track evidence, then converts those terms into a wide-area cue-management decision rather than a final classifier. That distinction is standard in the tracking literature: detection, tracking, cueing, and classification are related but separate tasks, and fusing them as if they were the same would overstate what the human method is actually doing \cite{hall1997,barshalom2001,blackman1999,mahler2007}.

The non-radar branches fill out the multimodal side of the ladder. The acoustic cue branch measures cadence, stability, and node agreement, which makes it useful for propulsion-pattern confirmation without turning the score into a proxy for identity, payload, or location. The passive-RF branch is intentionally sparse and provenance-heavy: it tracks no-signal behavior, burst contamination, clock offset, and source quality so the analyst can downweight corrupted RF context without inventing emitter-level truth from absence alone. The tabular and sequence ML controls are ordinary learned baselines that consume detector-facing features only; the denylist keeps them from seeing labels, split keys, group IDs, or audit-only generator state. In other words, they are stress-tested controls for the same evidence contract, not shortcuts around it.

At the top of the ladder sits accepted human fusion. This lane combines branch confidence, source agreement, stale-track penalties, and cross-sensor confirmation under a train/CV-calibrated threshold, which is why it is the correct human comparator for EI rather than a strawman \cite{hall1997,barshalom2001,mahler2007,guo2017}. The fusion step is intentionally conservative: it only accepts a detection when the branch evidence is mutually supportive and the calibration lock has been fixed before holdout scoring. Table~\ref{tab:humanmethods} gives the summary contract for each method, and Figure~\ref{fig:phaseladder} shows that the same ordering is used in the take-off, climb, and cruise windows.

\section{Engineered Intelligence}
Human radar engineering is powerful, but it has three bottlenecks: knowledge consumption, idea generation, and validation bandwidth. EI is the engineering response to those bottlenecks. It is not an opaque model handle. In this benchmark it is controlled algorithm discovery: candidate score families are generated, sparse combinations are ranked on train/CV evidence, a monotone calibrator is fit, a selection lock is written, and the blind holdout is scored once.

\subsection{Method Math}
Let $x_i$ be the detector-view feature vector for record $i$. Candidate component $j$ maps that view to a bounded score
\begin{equation}
s_{ij}=g_j(x_i).
\end{equation}
EI then searches sparse nonnegative late fusion weights,
\begin{equation}
z_i=\sum_{j=1}^{k}w_js_{ij},\qquad w_j\geq0,\qquad \sum_{j=1}^{k}w_j=1.
\end{equation}
The selected fused score is calibrated through monotone geodesic odds,
\begin{equation}
\hat p_i=\sigma\!\left(a\log\frac{z_i+\epsilon}{1-z_i+\epsilon}+b\right).
\end{equation}
The train/CV objective follows the implementation: it rewards AP and ROC AUC while penalizing excess FPR and weak initial-take-up behavior,
\begin{multline}
J=\mathrm{AP}_{\mathrm{CV}}+0.25\,\mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{CV}}\\
-\alpha\,\max(0,\mathrm{FPR}_{\mathrm{CV}}-\tau)-\beta\,\Delta_{\mathrm{takeup}}.
\end{multline}
All terms are computed on train/CV rows. Holdout rows are not used to choose candidates, calibrators, thresholds, components, or weights.

\section{Why It Matters}
The result matters because it changes the detector-development loop. Classical radar work still matters: without plausible waveform assumptions, detector views, split hygiene, calibration, and accepted fusion, there is no trustworthy benchmark. EI does not replace that foundation. It compounds it.

Once the problem is decomposed into auditable stages, the system can search for transforms, combinations, and calibrators that a human team might not propose first. The final artifact remains code, weights, math, and evidence. The invention process becomes continuous. Progress is then limited less by a fixed menu of manually enumerated ideas and more by simulator quality, evidence quality, compute, search discipline, and holdout hygiene.

\section{Limitations}
The corpus is synthetic, single-seed, and public-proxy. It has \MetricPositiveGroups{} positive groups total and \MetricHoldoutPositiveGroups{} positive groups in the holdout split. Group-block intervals are necessary but not enough for broad claims. EchoForge does not provide measured Iranian-drone radar truth, fielded-sensor equivalence, hardware-in-the-loop validation, track-level operational FAR, route/evasion modeling, or named-platform performance. The jamming/deception lane is defensive robustness stress only: it forbids jammer power, J/S, burn-through, timing recipes, exact ECCM behavior, evasion optimization, and field-performance claims. The ROC AUC guardrail also matters: accepted human fusion ranks better globally, while EI performs better at the low-FPR operating point chosen as the benchmark KPI.

The next evidence steps are multi-seed corpora, larger positive holdouts, richer weather/RFI/site stressors, measured compare-only anchors expanded by sensor branch, hardware-in-the-loop hooks, and a formal pre-registration file for every promoted EI search.

\section{Conclusion}
EchoForge provides a strict-open radar simulation and evidence stack for public-proxy UAS detection: explicit assumptions, radar branch cards, hard negatives, detector-view denylisting, group-locked splits, train/CV-only selection, and reproducible evidence. The paper's central result is simple: accepted human sensor fusion is strong, but EI-discovered sparse calibrated fusion is much stronger at the low-FPR operating point on this benchmark. EI raises \PrimaryKPI{} from \MetricPriorLcb{} to \MetricEiLcb{} and cuts selected-threshold false positives from \MetricPriorSelectedFP{} to \MetricEiSelectedFP{}. The future implied by this result is not a one-off model; it is a radar-development system in which novel math and code evolve in response to data under strict evidence controls.
