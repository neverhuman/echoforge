I reviewed the full 8-page PDF, including the rendered figures and tables. Overall: the paper has a strong reproducibility/claim-boundary concept, and the “synthetic, strict-open, compare-only anchor” framing is a real strength. The biggest issues are that the radar physics and evaluation protocol are not yet described tightly enough for a radar reviewer, the positive holdout is very small, several figures are visually overloaded or misleading, and a few metric/figure/table inconsistencies will be flagged immediately. 

## Executive assessment

**What the paper does well**

The paper is unusually careful about not overclaiming. It repeatedly states that EchoForge is synthetic public-proxy evidence, not measured-platform truth or operational sensor performance. That is excellent and important.

The group-locked split, detector-view denylisting, canary checks, label-shuffle sanity test, metadata-only baseline, and nearest-neighbor train/holdout scan are exactly the right instincts for a synthetic benchmark. The paper understands that leakage is the main danger.

The low-FPR KPI is appropriate for counter-UAS radar. Optimizing only ROC AUC would be weak for this problem; choosing recall at FPR ≤ 1% is much closer to how a radar system is judged in practice.

The false-alarm family idea is one of the best parts of the paper. Breaking false alarms into bird, RC fixed-wing, weather, RFI, clutter-only, multipath, terrain glint, etc. gives the reviewer a real operational interpretation rather than just a leaderboard score.

The compare-only measured-anchor framing is also good. Using KTH and other public datasets only as distribution-shape references, without claiming truth transfer, is the right boundary.

**Most important gaps**

The current manuscript reads more like an evidence/governance report than a fully mature radar benchmark paper. The radar signal chain needs more physical detail: waveform construction, range-Doppler processing, Doppler/velocity resolution, clutter model, RCS prior, multipath model, SNR/noise model, CFAR or detection baseline, and how acoustic/passive-RF cues are actually generated.

The holdout has only **8 positive groups / 24 positive records**. That makes the headline recall and phase-wise metrics fragile. The result may be promising, but the uncertainty must be shown much more aggressively.

There are metric consistency issues. Table IV reports selected AP = 0.824845, while Figure 5 appears to show selected holdout AP around 0.894. Also, the Figure 3 label “LCB95 Recall@1%FPR: 0.699” looks suspiciously close to the lower AP confidence bound in Table IV, not obviously the lower confidence bound of recall. These must be reconciled.

Some figures are too dense to read in IEEE two-column format. Figure 2 is smashed. Figure 3 has overlapping titles and too-small method labels. Figure 5 is too small to support the ablation claim. Figure 6 is currently a visual mess. Figure 8 compares heterogeneous units on one bar axis, which is not defensible.

Table V is the weakest table. It exposes audit handles, but the names are unreadable, some strings appear malformed, and the scientific meaning of the components is not clear.

## Section II: Radar and Multimodal Generative Model

This section is important, but it needs the most technical strengthening.

### What works

The paper includes a radar equation and a complex IQ equation. That gives the paper a radar-first foundation.

Table I gives nominal bands, bandwidths, PRF/CPI, pulse count, range bins, and range resolution. The range resolutions are physically consistent with the stated bandwidths: 600 MHz gives about 0.25 m, 180 MHz gives about 0.83 m, and 300 MHz gives about 0.5 m.

The paper lists receiver impairments: AGC compression, clock drift, quantization, dropped CPIs, PRF ambiguity, Doppler folding, calibration offset, and multipath masking. That is a good set.

### Major gaps

The waveform description is ambiguous. Figure 1 says “multibranch FMCW-style public-proxy radar,” but the text and tables talk about PRF, CPI, and pulses. For FMCW, the paper should explicitly use chirps, chirp repetition interval, sweep bandwidth, chirp duration, beat-frequency processing, range FFT, Doppler FFT, and windowing. “Pulses” is acceptable shorthand, but the signal chain should be internally consistent.

The complex IQ model is too simple for micro-Doppler. It appears to model a phase-stable target term plus clutter/noise, but a UAS micro-Doppler proxy should usually be a sum over scatterers with time-varying radial velocities: airframe, propeller, rotor/prop blade tips, glints, and aspect-dependent scattering centers. If the model is intentionally simpler, say so clearly.

The paper does not report Doppler resolution or velocity resolution. With 4 kHz PRF and 6 ms CPI, the Doppler-bin spacing is about 167 Hz. At X/Ku, that corresponds roughly to 2.4-2.8 m/s velocity spacing; at S-band, roughly 7.6-8.6 m/s. That is coarse for fine micro-Doppler interpretation. The paper should either justify this as a coarse proxy or increase/aggregate CPI for micro-Doppler features.

The paper does not specify SNR distributions, receiver noise temperature/noise figure, clutter-to-noise ratio, interference-to-noise ratio, RCS priors, aspect grids, target kinematics, altitude profile distributions, or target radial velocity distributions. These matter a lot.

The clutter model is too vague. Low-altitude radar performance is dominated by terrain clutter, grazing angle, multipath, weather, birds, wind turbines, RFI, and moving ground objects. The paper lists some of these as hard-negative families, but the generative assumptions need to be more explicit.

The acoustic and passive-RF cues are very underspecified. “Node-wise spectral cadence” and “provenance-quality geometry” are not enough. The reader needs sampling assumptions, node geometry, timing uncertainty, SNR, environmental noise, RF no-signal model, RFI burst process, and what features are actually exposed.

### Recommended additions

Add a compact subsection called **“Signal-chain implementation details.”** Include:

* waveform type per branch;
* chirp or pulse timing;
* sample rate or effective range-bin construction;
* range FFT and Doppler FFT processing;
* windowing;
* coherent/noncoherent integration;
* clutter generation;
* target scatterer model;
* RCS/scintillation prior;
* SNR/CNR/INR ranges;
* impairment parameter ranges;
* feature extraction;
* detector-facing feature schema.

Add formulas for:

* range resolution: (\Delta R = c/(2B));
* Doppler resolution: (\Delta f_D = 1/T_{\mathrm{CPI}});
* velocity resolution: (\Delta v = \lambda/(2T_{\mathrm{CPI}}));
* approximate unambiguous Doppler/velocity from PRF.

This would make the model card much more radar-reviewer friendly.

## Section IV: Evaluation Protocol and Calibration

### What works

The evaluation section chooses the right families of metrics: AP, ROC AUC, fixed-FPR recall, precision/recall/F1, Brier, ECE, and group-block bootstrap.

Using group-block bootstrap rather than row bootstrap is correct because the three phase records from one scenario group are correlated.

The primary KPI, LCB95 Recall@1%FPR, is conceptually good.

### Major gaps

The calibration equation needs clarification. The paper says the fused score is a nonnegative weighted sum of component scores, then applies a monotone log-odds transform. That only makes sense if (z_i) is clipped or guaranteed to lie in ([0,1]). If component scores are raw, calibrated, beta-transformed, or geodesic-odds values, then the score scale must be defined.

A monotone calibration should not change rank metrics such as AP or ROC AUC. But Figure 5 appears to show “no calibration” AP higher than the selected AP. If the calibration is truly monotone and applied after fusion, AP should be unchanged. If AP changes, the “no-calibration” ablation is doing more than removing a monotone calibration. This needs explanation.

The paper does not clearly state threshold selection. Was the threshold chosen on train/CV to hit 1% FPR? Was it selected from the ROC curve? Is Table IV using the same threshold as the 1% FPR recall? If not, label the counts as “default operating point” and provide a separate fixed-FPR confusion matrix.

Table IV has a potential inconsistency: baseline FP = 49 out of 4,476 negatives, which is about 1.1% FPR, not ≤1%. The selected candidate has 1 FP, which is about 0.02% FPR, far below 1%. If these are not fixed-FPR operating-point counts, the table should say so.

The paper reports AP confidence intervals but not the confidence interval for the actual primary KPI. If LCB95 Recall@1%FPR is the headline, Table IV must show the point estimate and lower confidence bound for that exact metric.

Add a table like this:

| Method | Threshold source | Threshold | TP | FP | TN | FN | FPR | Recall | Recall@1%FPR | LCB95 Recall@1%FPR | AP | AP CI | ROC AUC | Brier | ECE |
| ------ | ---------------- | --------: | -: | -: | -: | -: | --: | -----: | -----------: | -----------------: | -: | ----- | ------: | ----: | --: |

Also add:

* number of bootstrap replicates;
* resampling unit;
* whether positive groups are resampled with replacement;
* confidence interval type;
* calibration bin count and binning method;
* exact ECE formula.

# Standard visual/table style I recommend

Use a consistent, professional visual language across the paper:

**Colors**

* Selected / EchoForge candidate / EI: deep blue.
* Prior baseline: muted gray or muted green.
* Other baselines: light gray.
* Pass status: soft green.
* Warning/diagnostic: soft amber.
* Prohibited/failed boundary: soft red.
* Modalities: radar = blue, acoustic = purple, passive RF = teal, metadata/audit = gray.

Keep colors color-blind safe and readable in grayscale. Avoid rainbow colormaps.

**Tables**

Use IEEE-style clean tables:

* no vertical lines;
* booktabs-style horizontal rules;
* aligned decimals;
* integer counts with no decimals;
* AP/ROC/Recall/FPR to 3 decimals;
* CIs as `[lower, upper]`;
* bold best values;
* use light cell shading only for grouped interpretation, not decoration.

**Figures**

* Export as vector PDF/SVG whenever possible.
* Minimum final font size: about 7-8 pt after IEEE scaling.
* Use `figure*` for dense diagnostic figures.
* Do not put more than 4 panels in a single figure unless it spans two columns.
* Put exact values on bars when values are close.
* For raw counts, usually start axes at zero.
* For close performance comparisons, use a zoomed inset or clearly labeled truncated axis.
* For range-Doppler plots, use physical axes and units.
