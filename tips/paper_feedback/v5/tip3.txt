## What is still broken

The current repo already has some good infrastructure: `generate_figures_major_upgrade_v2.py` imports `textwrap`, writes vector PDFs for most figures, and has `FIG_DPI = 450`; the TeX file also now uses full-width `figure*` for several dashboards. But the renderer still uses 10-inch figures such as `10.1 x 5.6`, `10.5 x 5.8`, `10.3 x 4.9`, `10.4 x 5.8`, and `10.1 x 5.1`, then the paper shrinks them into IEEE width. That is why the final PDF text becomes tiny and collisions remain.   

The worst visual failure is **Figure 6**. In the rendered PDF, the model-card panel still has cue-definition text and boundary text colliding, with wasted vertical white space above it. The current function still builds this as a 2-by-2 layout with receiver impairments and cue definitions sharing the same lower-right axis, which is exactly the wrong structure for that content.  

**Figure 7** is better than Figure 6 but still not tier-1: the row labels and y-axis label compete, and the heatmap/colorbar layout is too cramped. The current function uses a label column plus three heatmaps, but then uses `fig.colorbar(image, ax=fig.axes, ...)`, which lets the colorbar negotiate against every axis and worsens the layout.  

**Figure 4 and Figure 5 are over-dense.** Figure 4 still has a huge multi-family visual burden; the right panel should show false-positive burden by method and top family, while the detailed family accounting belongs in the tables. Figure 5 is scientifically useful, but the labels need more horizontal breathing room and smaller, consistent print-size typography.  

One important claim-flow issue: Table IX shows that the passive-RF-only view has higher AP and swept Recall@≤1%FPR than the full locked candidate, even though its F1 and ECE are worse. The paper must explicitly say the locked candidate is the **pre-registered, calibrated, selected-threshold artifact**, not a universal dominator of every post-hoc view control.
