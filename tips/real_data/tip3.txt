This is a **very strong fit for EchoForge**—but the trick is to separate datasets that are good for **radar perception ML** from datasets that are actually useful for **simulator validation**.

EchoForge’s repo describes the project as a “radar-first, GPU-native synthetic sensing foundry” for public-proxy signatures, radar-chain products, validation reports, and benchmark packages; it also distinguishes true **V5 measured validation** as comparison against lawful measured data with explicit provenance. That means the best datasets are not just “large,” but datasets that preserve raw ADC, radar cubes/tensors, phase history, calibration, timestamps, and sensor parameters. ([GitHub][1])

**My recommendation:** start with **RaDICaL, UW Raw ADC Automotive, RADIal, UW Carry-Object 2D-MIMO, K-Radar, S2R-Bench, GOTCHA/SAMPLE/MSTAR, CARRADA/RADDet/CRUW, Oxford/Boreas/ColoRadar, RaDelft, and View-of-Delft**. Those give you the best mix of true measured radar, manageable scene recreation, calibration metadata, and lots of samples.

“Free” below means **free/open/free-to-request for research**, not necessarily permissive for commercial redistribution. Licenses vary a lot.

## Controlled micro-Doppler / small-scene datasets

These are excellent for **unit tests** because they are much easier to reproduce than full autonomous-driving scenes.

| Dataset                                 | Best EchoForge use                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **KTH drones/birds/humans 77 GHz FMCW** | 75,868 radar samples from birds, humans, six drone types, 77 GHz FMCW mechanically scanned radar, CC BY 4.0. Fantastic for micro-Doppler and target-signature validation. ([Zenodo][32])                                           |
| **Open Radar Initiative datasets**      | Ground-surveillance dataset with stationary radar, people/bicycles/UAV/vehicles, Doppler spectra, range, azimuth, velocity, SNR, class name, and radar parameters. Excellent for Doppler-track reproduction. ([GitHub][33])        |
| **CI4R-MULTI3**                         | Multi-frequency human activity data from 10 GHz UWB, 24 GHz FMCW, and 77 GHz FMCW radars, 11 activities/gaits, synchronized colocated sensors. Good for cross-frequency EchoForge signature checks. ([GitHub][34])                 |
| **RAD-DAR real Doppler database**       | Kaggle dataset with 17k+ Doppler samples of drones, cars, and people. Good for Doppler classifier benchmarking, though not as rich as raw ADC/cube datasets. ([Kaggle][35])                                                        |
| **mmWave vital-sign raw ADC dataset**   | TI AWR1642 + DCA1000 raw ADC for vital-sign monitoring across distance, angle, orientation, elevated heart/respiration, asthma, meditation, etc. Useful if EchoForge supports human micro-motion/vital-sign sensing. ([arXiv][36]) |

## SAR / remote-sensing radar datasets worth adding later

These are huge and real, but not necessarily “easy” to recreate unless EchoForge has SAR/orbital/airborne modes.

| Dataset                  | Why it matters                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **ATRNet-STAR**          | New large-scale SAR vehicle dataset with 40 target types, almost 200k target images, complex floating-point data, processed amplitude data, raw full-scene SAR products, metadata, and quad-polarization releases. ([GitHub][37])                        |
| **Capella Open SAR**     | Open high-resolution commercial SAR scenes on AWS, CC BY 4.0, new data added quarterly. Good for modern commercial SAR imagery validation. ([Registry of Open Data][38])                                                                                 |
| **Umbra Open SAR**       | Open SAR data with very high-resolution imagery, SICD/SIDD/CPHD-style products and metadata, time-series sites. Great for phase-history/SAR product comparisons. ([Umbra • We Know Space][39])                                                           |
| **Sentinel-1**           | Free, full, open C-band SAR access; enormous sample volume, all-weather day/night Earth observation. Great for broad SAR texture/statistics, less for object-level 1-to-1 unless scenes are carefully selected. ([Registry of Open Data][40])            |
| **NASA UAVSAR**          | NASA airborne repeat-track polarimetric L-band SAR, useful for deformation, vegetation, hydrology, ice, and other repeat-pass SAR cases. ([NASA Earthdata][41])                                                                                          |
| **NOAA NEXRAD Level II** | Massive public Doppler weather-radar archive, original-resolution Level II data, new data added continuously, public use. Only relevant if EchoForge will model weather radar volumes rather than automotive/object radar. ([Registry of Open Data][42]) |

## What I would implement first in EchoForge

**Phase 1: Raw ADC / phase-history adapters.**
Build loaders for RaDICaL, UW Raw ADC Automotive, RADIal, UW Carry-Object, GOTCHA, and SAMPLE/MSTAR. These give you the most honest low-level comparison because you can run both real and simulated data through the same FFT / beamforming / CFAR / image-formation chain.

**Phase 2: Tensor and cube adapters.**
Add K-Radar, CARRADA, RADDet, CRUW, RaDelft, and possibly Zendar. Compare RAD, RA, RD, RAED, point-cloud, and object-level outputs. This is the sweet spot for “many samples” without requiring full ADC access.

**Phase 3: Scenario-scale adapters.**
Add S2R-Bench, Dual Radar, View-of-Delft, RadarScenes, RADIATE, Oxford, Boreas, ColoRadar, NTU4DRadLM, nuScenes, ZOD, MSC RAD4R, and V2X-Radar. These are best for scene realism, weather, road geometry, traffic distributions, and perception benchmarks.

## Metrics that make the comparison honest

For **raw ADC datasets**, compare after a shared processing chain: range-Doppler peak error, range-angle peak error, Doppler marginal distributions, noise floor, clutter statistics, CFAR detections, and per-object residuals.

For **radar tensors/cubes**, compare RA/RD/RAD/RAED maps using dB-domain error, normalized cross-correlation, SSIM-like image metrics, peak voxel agreement, object-conditioned intensity distributions, and false-positive clutter.

For **radar point clouds**, do not claim EM-level validation. Use object range/azimuth/elevation/Doppler residuals, point-count distributions, Chamfer distance, BEV occupancy IoU, detection mAP, and tracking consistency.

For **SAR**, compare phase history where available, then formed imagery: impulse response, speckle statistics, target chip similarity, polarimetric consistency, and synthetic-vs-measured class transfer.

## The strongest shortlist

Start here:

1. **RaDICaL** — easiest raw ADC controlled benchmark.
2. **UW Raw ADC Automotive** — easiest raw ADC automotive benchmark.
3. **RADIal** — best high-definition automotive raw-radar benchmark.
4. **UW Carry-Object 2D-MIMO** — controlled 192-virtual-element MIMO benchmark.
5. **K-Radar** — best full 4D radar tensor dataset.
6. **S2R-Bench** — best simulator-vs-real anomaly benchmark.
7. **GOTCHA + SAMPLE + MSTAR** — best SAR measured/synthetic validation suite.
8. **CARRADA + RADDet + CRUW** — best mid-level RAD/RA/RD automotive benchmarks.
9. **Oxford + Boreas + ColoRadar + NTU4DRadLM** — best radar-SLAM / localization pool.
10. **View-of-Delft + RadarScenes + RADIATE + Dual Radar + V2X-Radar** — best high-volume scenario pool.

One caveat: datasets such as nuScenes, RadarScenes, View-of-Delft, ZOD, and many 4D radar sets are extremely useful, but they are often **vendor-filtered radar point clouds**, not raw electromagnetic measurements. They should validate EchoForge’s **post-processed perception products**, not the actual RF chain.
